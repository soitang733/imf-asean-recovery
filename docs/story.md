# Similar shocks and diverging recoveries in ASEAN

## Central finding

The data do not identify a single unconditional “winner.” They reveal different combinations of recovery and cost. The clearest comparative story is Lao P.D.R. and Singapore: their 2020 COVID shocks relative to pre-COVID growth were similar, approximately -6.85 and -6.72 percentage points, but their 2021–2024 recovery gaps diverged to -3.35 and +2.12 percentage points.

## Evidence chain

Singapore’s post-COVID average growth moved above its 2015–2019 baseline, while Lao P.D.R. returned to positive growth but remained well below its earlier pace. Lao P.D.R. also recorded an inflation cost of about 18.46 percentage points and a debt cost of about 25.55 percentage points of GDP. These indicators appeared together; the analysis does not claim that one caused another.

## Why this is surprising

The similar initial growth shock might suggest similar recovery paths. Instead, the later paths are far apart. This makes the comparison stronger than simply naming the country with the highest post-COVID growth rate.

## Alternative explanations

Economic structure, tourism exposure, commodity prices, exchange-rate dynamics, reopening timing, fiscal space and domestic policy differed substantially. Myanmar is another major outlier, but its post-2020 path cannot reasonably be attributed to COVID alone.

## Important measurement caveat

Singapore’s gross-debt increase must not be read as a standard solvency signal. Singapore’s Ministry of Finance states that the government has a strong net asset position and that most borrowing is not used to finance spending. This is why the main story emphasizes separate indicators rather than an arbitrary composite score.

## Limitations

- Period averages hide turning points and base effects.
- WEO data can be revised and some 2024 observations may be estimates.
- Unemployment is unavailable for three ASEAN-10 economies in the COVID window.
- Gross debt is not net debt and institutional definitions matter.
- Descriptive co-movement is not causal evidence.
